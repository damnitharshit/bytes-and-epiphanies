# Exercise 1.33

You can obtain an even more general version of `accumulate` (Exercise 1.32) by introducing the notion of a filter on the terms to be combined. That is, combine only those terms derived from values in the range that satisfy a specified condition. The resulting `filtered-accumulate` abstraction takes the same arguments as `accumulate`, together with an additional predicate of one argument that specifies the filter. Write `filtered-accumulate` as a procedure. Show how to express the following using `filtered-accumulate`:

### a.
The sum of the squares of the prime numbers in the interval `a` to `b` (assuming that you have a `prime?` predicate already written).

### b. 
The product of all the positive integers less than `n` that are relatively prime to `n` (i.e., all positive integers `i < n` such that `GCD(i, n) = 1`).

## Solution:

- filtered accumulate:
```scheme
(define (filtered-accumulate combiner filter null-value term a next b)
  (if (> a b)
      null-value
      (cond
        [(filter a) (combiner (term a) (accumulate accumulater null-value term (next a) next b))]
        [else (accumulate combiner null-value term (next a) next b)])))
```

- sum of squared primes
```scheme
(define (sum-of-squared-primes a b)

  (define (square x)
    (* x x))

  (define (inc x)
    (+ x 1))

  (filtered-accumulate prime? + 0 square a inc b))
```

- Product of smaller relative primes
```scheme
(define (product-of-smaller-relative-primes n)

  (define (id x) x)

  (define (inc x) (+ x 1))

  (define (gcd a b)
    (if (= b 0) a (gcd b (remainder a b))))

  (define (relatively-prime? a)
    (= (gcd n a) 1))
    
  (filtered-accumulate relatively-prime? * 1 id 0 inc n))
```
